# Community Code of Conduct

## 1. Our Commitment

We commit to building a welcoming, safe, and equitable community where every person can
participate with dignity and belonging. We value each contributor regardless of age, body size,
disability, ethnicity, caste, color, race, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression,
sexual orientation, language, religion or philosophical belief, nationality or social origin,
socio-economic status, education, appearance, neurodiversity or physical characteristics, and
experience level. Everyone who participates in good faith deserves the same chance to be heard,
to learn, and to contribute. We also recognize that words land differently across cultures and
backgrounds, so we listen for impact as much as intent and stay curious about what we may have
missed.

## 2. How We Try to Show Up (Acceptable Behavior)

We try to make this a place people want to return to. In that spirit, we aim to:

- Honor the purpose of the community and keep conversations relevant and constructive.
- Engage kindly and honestly, assuming good faith while staying open to being wrong.
- Welcome different viewpoints and lived experiences, even when they challenge our own.
- Take responsibility for the impact of our words and actions, and make room to adjust.
- Give and receive feedback with grace, focusing on ideas and shared goals.
- Choose repair when harm happens, preferring apology and learning over defensiveness.
- Care for the collective well-being of the community above individual preference.

## 3. What Crosses a Line (Unacceptable Behavior)

The following behaviors are not acceptable in our community:

- Harassment or continued attention after someone has set a clear boundary.
- Demeaning remarks or attacks on a person's character, competence, or belonging.
- Stereotyping, slurs, or discrimination based on any aspect of identity.
- Sexualized language, imagery, or behavior where it is unwelcome or out of place.
- Sharing someone's private information without their consent.
- Threats, encouragement of harm, or sustained disruption that undermines safety.

## 4. Where This Applies (Scope of Application)

This code applies in all community spaces — issues, pull requests, discussions, chat, and events
— and whenever someone is officially representing the community, such as when using an official
email address, posting from an official social account, or acting as an appointed representative
at an online or offline gathering.

## 5. If Something Happens (Reporting Procedures)

Not every disagreement is a violation, but if something feels off, please tell us early so we can
help before it grows. You can reach the maintainers at **<azelann.borde@gmail.com>**. For
private or security-sensitive reports, open a
[draft security advisory](https://github.com/SirEriTheRed/ts-bastion/security/advisories/new)
instead of a public issue. Every report is reviewed promptly and fairly. We respect the privacy
and safety of everyone involved, do not tolerate retaliation, keep enforcement details private,
and share only what is needed to keep people safe.

## 6. How We Respond (Enforcement Guidelines)

We take a repair-first approach. Most situations are best resolved with conversation, and formal
steps are rarely needed. When they are, we use discretion and may skip lower steps if harm is
serious. Consequences are thoughtfully applied and focus on understanding, repair, and preventing
recurrence.

### 1. Gentle Notice

**What happened:** Language or behavior that felt unwelcome or out of step with this code.

**What follows:** A private, written note explaining why it was a concern and how to adjust.

**How repair can look:** Acknowledging impact, a brief apology, and choosing a different approach
going forward.

### 2. Limited Participation

**What happened:** A single incident or a short pattern that caused clear harm or discomfort.

**What follows:** A time-limited pause from interacting with involved people, including
unsolicited contact in community spaces or outside channels like social media.

**How repair can look:** Reflection on what happened, a sincere apology if appropriate, and
agreement to conditions for returning to full participation.

### 3. Temporary Suspension

**What happened:** A serious breach or ongoing behavior after earlier feedback.

**What follows:** A temporary suspension from community interaction and communication, with a
defined return date and clear expectations shared privately.

**How repair can look:** Demonstrated understanding of impact, commitment to changed behavior, and
respecting the boundaries set during the suspension.

### 4. Removal

**What happened:** A sustained pattern of harmful behavior, harassment of an individual, or
aggression toward a group.

**What follows:** Permanent removal from public participation in the community.

**How repair can look:** In rare cases, return may be considered only after genuine accountability
and credible assurance that harm will not recur.
